Additional analysis requirements for DAComp-006

Investigate which aspects account for instability in South China's monthly
total profit. Follow the analysis stages below, using the actual query results
to choose subsequent hypotheses and drill-downs. The stages specify the depth
of evidence expected, not a fixed SQL sequence or predetermined conclusions.

For this task, perform data processing and quantitative analysis in the database
whenever they can be expressed in SQL. This includes filtering, grouping,
aggregation, joins, ranking, ratios, month-to-month comparisons, volatility and
contribution calculations. Do not export base-table data for local analysis or
use Python, pandas, NumPy or another external calculation tool as an alternative.
Use the supplied DB tools; the Agent selects hypotheses and follow-up queries,
interprets returned evidence and writes the report.

Return compact analytical results and targeted detail rows needed for a check.
When a result is still too large to inspect, first consider further filtering,
aggregation or ranking in SQL. Do not retrieve millions of rows for processing
outside the database. Inspect additional result pages when necessary to support
a conclusion.

1. Establish the scope. Verify time coverage and how destinations encode the
   requested region. Check the available revenue, cost, quantity and profit
   measures. Derive dates, region selections and any data-quality concerns from
   observed values; do not assume specific anomalies or a particular date range.

2. Quantify monthly instability. Obtain the monthly profit series and compute
   its mean, standard deviation, coefficient of variation, range and month-to-
   month changes in SQL. State the variance convention, handle zero denominators
   and missing months explicitly, and select important changes from these results.

3. Examine revenue, cost and volume explanations. Compare monthly orders,
   quantity, revenue, profit and cost components. Distinguish total-volume
   changes from changes per order or per unit. Check discounts, value-added
   revenue and weighted margins where relevant. Quantify the evidence before
   identifying a factor as important or dismissing it.

4. Examine product composition. Calculate product-by-month measures and
   product-level volatility in SQL. Compare absolute contributions to selected
   monthly changes with relative volatility, so a small volatile category is
   not automatically treated as the main driver. Use the results to select any
   further product/month investigation.

5. Examine geography. Derive suitable regional and city groupings from the
   actual destination values. Compare monthly contributions and volatility,
   and investigate important segments selected from those results. If the data
   reveal inconsistent regional labels, quantify their effect rather than
   assuming a specific province or correction in advance.

6. Verify anomalies when evidence warrants it. If aggregate checks reveal
   unusual margins, inconsistent amounts or signs of concentration, inspect
   relevant individual records. Quantify whether they materially affect total
   profit changes or instead distort a ratio. If the evidence does not justify
   a drill-down, explain why and omit it.

7. Synthesize and verify. Rank the supported explanations. For each major
   conclusion, identify the supporting query result and the numerical evidence.
   Resolve material competing explanations through targeted checks when needed;
   retain uncertainty when the data cannot distinguish them. Associations alone
   do not establish a causal explanation such as a holiday effect.

Compute the quantitative statistics used as evidence in SQL and inspect the
returned results. Do not substitute manual/model arithmetic for the standard
deviations, coefficients of variation or contribution calculations reported as
measured evidence. Ordinary presentation rounding is allowed. If the database
cannot compute a required statistic reliably, state the limitation and revise
the analysis; do not silently replace it with model arithmetic or local code.

Queries whose choices depend on earlier results must be planned after examining
those results. Independent measures may be combined in one SQL statement; reuse
available evidence where sufficient. Do not reproduce avoidable retries or
repeated queries. There is no target or minimum number of SQL calls. Existing
tool limits still apply.
