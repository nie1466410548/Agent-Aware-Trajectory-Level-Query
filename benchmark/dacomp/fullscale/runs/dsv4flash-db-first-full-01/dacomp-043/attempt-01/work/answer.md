# Hand, Foot, and Mouth Disease (HFMD): Comparative Analysis of Deceased vs Recovered Populations

## Executive Summary

This analysis examines 211 distinct HFMD cases (56 deceased, 155 recovered) across symptoms, prevention/control measures, and treatment dimensions. The results reveal that the deceased population differed significantly from the recovered population in several key areas: **higher prevalence of pneumonia complications, lower body temperature at presentation, more frequent vomiting and headache symptoms, vesicular rash on limbs, greater diagnostic delay, lower diagnostic quality scores, and notably lower medical insurance reimbursement rates.**

---

## 1. Symptoms

### 1.1 Body Temperature

The deceased group had a **significantly lower** mean highest body temperature (38.02°C) compared to the recovered group (38.70°C) (Mann-Whitney U test, p=0.0036). This counterintuitive finding may reflect more severe systemic involvement without a robust febrile response, or potentially delayed presentation after the febrile phase.

| Metric | Deceased (n=58 records) | Recovered (n=224 records) |
|--------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Mean Temperature | 38.02°C | 38.70°C |
| Min–Max | 36.0–39.9°C | 36.0–42.1°C |

![Temperature and Data Completeness](figure4_temperature_completeness.png)

### 1.2 Complication Type

The complication profile differed markedly between groups:

| Complication | Deceased (n=58) | Recovered (n=224) |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Pneumonia** | 23 (39.7%) | 36 (16.1%) |
| **Skin Infection** | 20 (34.5%) | 121 (54.0%) |
| **Encephalitis** | 15 (25.9%) | 67 (29.9%) |

Pneumonia was **2.5× more prevalent** in the deceased group, suggesting that lower respiratory tract involvement is a major risk factor for mortality.

### 1.3 Associated Symptoms

| Symptom | Deceased (n=58) | Recovered (n=224) |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Cough | 14 (24.1%) | 104 (46.4%) |
| Headache | 20 (34.5%) | 42 (18.8%) |
| Vomiting | 24 (41.4%) | 78 (34.8%) |

The deceased group showed a **higher proportion of headache and vomiting** (neurological/GI symptoms) and a **lower proportion of cough** compared to recovered patients.

### 1.4 Rash Characteristics

| Rash Distribution | Deceased (n=58) | Recovered (n=224) |
|------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Head & Face | 20 (34.5%) | 67 (29.9%) |
| Trunk | 16 (27.6%) | 115 (51.3%) |
| Limbs | 22 (37.9%) | 42 (18.8%) |

| Rash Morphology | Deceased (n=58) | Recovered (n=224) |
|----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Macule | 12 (20.7%) | 76 (33.9%) |
| Papule | 17 (29.3%) | 82 (36.6%) |
| **Vesicle** | **29 (50.0%)** | 66 (29.5%) |

The deceased group had a **predominantly limb distribution** and **vesicular morphology**, suggesting a more severe cutaneous manifestation.

### 1.5 Past Medical History

| Past Medical History | Deceased (n=56 cases) | Recovered (n=155 cases) |
|---------------------|----------------------|-------------------------|
| None | 19 (33.9%) | 75 (48.4%) |
| J01 (Respiratory disease) | 22 (39.3%) | 25 (16.1%) |
| B20 (HIV-related) | 15 (26.8%) | 55 (35.5%) |

The deceased group had a **significantly higher proportion of pre-existing respiratory disease (J01)** — 39.3% vs 16.1% — suggesting that underlying respiratory conditions may predispose to severe HFMD outcomes.

## 2. Prevention & Control

### 2.1 Vaccination Status

Vaccination coverage was nearly universal: among 245 cases with epidemiological investigation data, 239 (97.6%) had received 2 doses of inactivated vaccine, and 6 (2.4%) had received 1 dose. No unvaccinated cases were recorded. This high coverage precludes analysis of vaccination as a differentiating factor.

### 2.2 Isolation Status

| Isolation Status | Deceased (n=58 records) | Recovered (n=224 records) |
|-----------------|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| Isolated | 35 (60.3%) | 100 (44.6%) |
| Not Isolated | 21 (36.2%) | 124 (55.4%) |

The deceased group had a **higher isolation rate** (60.3%), likely reflecting the more severe clinical presentation requiring isolation.

### 2.3 Isolation Type (from isolation_and_disinfection records)

| Isolation Type | Deceased (n=15 records) | Recovered (n=95 records) |
|---------------|-------------------------|--------------------------|
| Home | 11 (73.3%) | 46 (48.4%) |
| Hospital | 4 (26.7%) | 18 (18.9%) |
| Centralized | 0 (0.0%) | 31 (32.6%) |

Notably, **no deceased cases were isolated in centralized facilities**, while 32.6% of recovered cases were. This may reflect differences in outbreak response or disease severity.

### 2.4 PPE Usage During Isolation

| PPE Status | Deceased (n=15) | Recovered (n=95) |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------|
| Complete | 9 (60.0%) | 66 (69.5%) |
| Missing | 6 (40.0%) | 29 (30.5%) |

The deceased group had a **higher proportion of missing PPE** (40.0% vs 30.5%), suggesting potential gaps in infection control measures.

### 2.5 Contact History

| Contact Location | Deceased (n=21 records) | Recovered (n=132 records) |
|-----------------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Hospital | 8 (38.1%) | 45 (34.1%) |
| School | 8 (38.1%) | 42 (31.8%) |
| Mall | 5 (23.8%) | 9 (6.8%) |
| Home | 0 (0.0%) | 36 (27.3%) |

Deceased cases had **no home contact history** and a **higher proportion of mall contacts** (23.8% vs 6.8%), possibly indicating different exposure patterns.

## 3. Treatment

### 3.1 Hospitalization and Treatment Type

| Metric | Deceased (n=58) | Recovered (n=224) |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Hospitalized | 27 (46.6%) | 92 (41.1%) |
| Antiviral Treatment | 24 (41.4%) | 94 (42.0%) |
| Symptomatic Treatment | 32 (55.2%) | 130 (58.0%) |

Treatment patterns were broadly similar between groups, though the deceased group had a slightly higher hospitalization rate.

### 3.2 Laboratory Tests

| Lab Test | Deceased (n=58) | Recovered (n=224) |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Antibody Test | 32 (55.2%) | 87 (38.8%) |
| Complete Blood Count | 26 (44.8%) | 137 (61.2%) |

The deceased group had a **higher proportion of antibody tests**, possibly reflecting more extensive diagnostic workup for severe cases.

### 3.3 Allergic Reaction Management

| Management | Deceased (n=58) | Recovered (n=224) |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Anti-allergy treatment | 30 (51.7%) | 100 (44.6%) |
| None | 28 (48.3%) | 124 (55.4%) |

The deceased group required **more anti-allergy treatment**, suggesting higher rates of allergic reactions.

### 3.4 Complication Management

| Severity | Deceased (n=27 records) | Recovered (n=135 records) |
|---------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Mild | 7 (25.9%) | 71 (52.6%) |
| Moderate | 13 (48.1%) | 46 (34.1%) |
| Severe | 7 (25.9%) | 18 (13.3%) |

The deceased group had **more moderate-to-severe complications** (74.1% vs 47.4%).

| Management Outcome | Deceased (n=27) | Recovered (n=135) |
|-------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Observation → Sequelae | 5 (18.5%) | 5 (3.7%) |
| Referral → Sequelae | 8 (29.6%) | 10 (7.4%) |
| Treatment → Cured | 6 (22.2%) | 8 (5.9%) |
| Treatment → Sequelae | 6 (22.2%) | 36 (26.7%) |

Deceased cases more frequently had **referral resulting in sequelae** (29.6% vs 7.4%).

### 3.5 Medical Insurance Reimbursement

| Metric | Deceased | Recovered |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| Avg Reimbursement Rate | **3.35%** | **58.64%** |
| Avg Treatment Cost | 3,748 | 4,352 |

This is one of the most striking findings: the deceased group had a **dramatically lower medical insurance reimbursement rate** (3.35% vs 58.64%). This could indicate significantly less financial protection, which may have contributed to delayed or inadequate care.

## 4. Diagnosis

### 4.1 Initial Diagnosis

| Diagnosis Type | Deceased (n=26 records) | Recovered (n=172 records) |
|---------------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Suspected | 7 (26.9%) | 96 (55.8%) |
| Clinical | 14 (53.8%) | 24 (14.0%) |
| Confirmed | 5 (19.2%) | 52 (30.2%) |

Deceased cases were **more likely to receive a Clinical diagnosis** (53.8% vs 14.0%) at initial assessment, suggesting they presented with more recognizable severe disease.

### 4.2 Diagnostic Delay

| Delay Reason | Deceased (n=26) | Recovered (n=172) |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| None | 10 (38.5%) | 115 (66.9%) |
| Patient delayed seeking attention | **16 (61.5%)** | 57 (33.1%) |

The deceased group had a **nearly 2× higher rate of patient-related diagnostic delay** (61.5% vs 33.1%), indicating that late presentation was a major contributor to mortality.

### 4.3 Diagnostic Quality

| Metric | Deceased (n=26) | Recovered (n=172) |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Mean Diagnostic Quality Score | **53.37** | **69.30** |
| Mean Compliance Rate | **0.56** | **0.75** |

Diagnostic quality scores were **significantly lower** for the deceased group (53.37 vs 69.30), suggesting that diagnostic processes may have been less thorough for these cases.

### 4.4 Case Classification

| Classification | Deceased (n=26) | Recovered (n=172) |
|---------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Sporadic | 9 (34.6%) | 101 (58.7%) |
| Outbreak | 4 (15.4%) | 21 (12.2%) |
| Sudden Event | **13 (50.0%)** | 50 (29.1%) |

Deceased cases were more frequently classified as **sudden events** (50.0% vs 29.1%), consistent with more acute presentation.

---

## 5. Data Quality

| Metric | Deceased (n=56) | Recovered (n=155) | p-value |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|---------|
| Mean Data Completeness Score | **50.68** | **62.63** | 0.0105 |

Records for deceased cases had **significantly lower data completeness scores**, which may indicate that data collection was less thorough for fatal cases, potentially introducing bias.

## Summary of Notable Differences

| Dimension | Key Finding |
|-----------|------------|
| **Symptoms** | Deceased: More pneumonia (39.7% vs 16.1%), more vomiting/headache, vesicular limb rash, lower body temperature (38.02 vs 38.70°C), more pre-existing respiratory disease (J01: 39.3% vs 16.1%) |
| **Prevention/Control** | Deceased: Higher isolation rate (60.3%), more PPE gaps (40% missing PPE), no centralized isolation, no home contact history, more mall contacts |
| **Treatment** | Deceased: Higher complication severity, more treatment resulting in sequelae, dramatically lower medical insurance reimbursement (3.35% vs 58.64%), more anti-allergy treatment |
| **Diagnosis** | Deceased: Higher diagnostic delay (61.5% vs 33.1% patient-delayed), lower diagnostic quality scores (53.37 vs 69.30), more sudden event classification |
| **Data Quality** | Deceased: Lower data completeness (50.68 vs 62.63, p=0.0105) |

## Limitations

1. **Sample size asymmetry**: The deceased group (56 cases, 58 records) is substantially smaller than the recovered group (155 cases, 224 records), limiting statistical power.
2. **Data completeness bias**: The lower data completeness scores for deceased cases may indicate missing data that could affect the reliability of findings.
3. **Observational nature**: This analysis identifies associations but cannot establish causal relationships.
4. **Multiple records per case**: Some cases had multiple clinical manifestation records with different outcomes, requiring case-level aggregation that may obscure some patterns.
5. **Limited vaccination variability**: Nearly all cases were vaccinated, preventing analysis of vaccination effectiveness.
6. **Reimbursement data**: The medical insurance reimbursement variable had very different distributions between groups; the low reimbursement in deceased cases may reflect confounding factors such as socioeconomic status or insurance type not captured in the data.
