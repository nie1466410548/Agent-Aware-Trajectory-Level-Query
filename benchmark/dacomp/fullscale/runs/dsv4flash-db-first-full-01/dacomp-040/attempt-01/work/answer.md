# China's Education Achievements 2000–2020: A Data-Based Summary

## Data & Method
Analysis is based on the 2000, 2010, and 2020 census tables of population aged 6+ (population aged 3+ in 2020, restricted to 6+ for comparability) cross-tabulated by age, sex, and educational attainment, plus urban/town/rural splits. Education categories differ across years (2000 includes "Literacy Class" and "Vocational School"; 2020 splits postgraduate into Master's and Doctoral), so levels were aggregated for comparability. Note: the 2020 column "ever Attended School – Total" is the never-attended count (validated against the male/female columns); the "Preschool" count for ages 6+ is treated separately.

## 1. National Level: Rapid Upgrading of the Whole Population's Educational Attainment

The population aged 6 and over grew from **1,156.7 million (2000)** to **1,242.5 million (2010)** and **1,315.3 million (2020)**. Over the same period the educational structure shifted decisively upward:

| Attainment level | 2000 (millions, %) | 2010 (millions, %) | 2020 (millions, %) |
|---|---|---|---|
| No schooling (incl. literacy class in 2000) | 110.4 (9.5%) | 62.1 (5.0%) | 41.5 (3.2%) |
| Primary school | 441.6 (38.2%) | 357.2 (28.7%) | 347.4 (26.4%) |
| Junior high school | 422.4 (36.5%) | 518.2 (41.7%) | 487.1 (37.0%) |
| Senior high school (incl. vocational in 2000) | 138.3 (12.0%) | 186.6 (15.0%) | 212.2 (16.1%) |
| Junior college | 29.0 (2.5%) | 68.6 (5.5%) | 112.3 (8.5%) |
| Undergraduate | 14.2 (1.2%) | 45.6 (3.7%) | 94.2 (7.2%) |
| Postgraduate (Master's + Doctoral) | 0.9 (0.08%) | 4.1 (0.33%) | 10.8 (0.82%) |
| **Higher education total (junior college+)** | **44.0 (3.8%)** | **118.4 (9.5%)** | **217.2 (16.5%)** |

**Key achievements:**
- **Illiteracy largely eliminated.** The never-attended population fell from 89.6 million (7.7%) in 2000 to 41.5 million (3.2%) in 2020 — a decline of ~54% in absolute numbers despite a growing population. The separate "Literacy Class" category (20.8 million people, 1.8% of the 6+ population in 2000) had essentially disappeared by 2010, evidence that mass literacy programmes succeeded.
- **Mass higher education.** The share of the population with junior college or above grew **~4.3×** (3.8% → 16.5%), the number of people with higher education grew from 44.0 million to **217.2 million (~4.9×)**. Undergraduates rose ~7× and postgraduates ~12× (0.88M → 10.8M).
- **Nine-year compulsory education became the norm.** Combined primary + junior high attainment (the basic-education tier) covered ~75% in 2000 and remains the modal tier (~63%), while the primary-only share dropped from 38% to 26% as children progressed further.

## 2. Gender Perspective: A Dramatic Narrowing of the Gender Gap

Women remained the majority of the never-attended (71.7% in 2000 and 74.2% in 2020) — a legacy of historical inequalities among the elderly — but the female share of every higher tier rose steadily:

| Level – female share | 2000 | 2010 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Undergraduate | 34.9% | 44.7% | **49.4%** |
| Junior college | 40.2% | 46.3% | 47.7% |
| Postgraduate | 30.2% | 43.2% | (Master's **50.2%**, Doctoral 39.4%) |

Higher-education participation rates (junior college+):
- Male: 4.57% (2000) → 10.18% (2010) → 16.65% (2020)
- Female: 3.00% (2000) → 8.85% (2010) → 16.37% (2020)

The male–female gap in higher-education attainment shrank from **1.6 percentage points in 2000 to just 0.3 points in 2020**, and at the undergraduate and Master's levels women now achieve **near or above gender parity**. This is one of the clearest educational achievements of the two decades.

## 3. Age Perspective: Cohort-on-Cohort Progress

Education levels by age group show continuous improvement for each successive generation:

- **School-age children (6–14):** the no-schooling rate fell from **2.7% (2000) → 2.1% (2010) → 0.5% (2020)** — compulsory education now reaches essentially all children.
- **Young adults (20–24):** the higher-education rate rose from **8.6% → 25.3% → 51.9%** — by 2020 more than half of young adults had college or above, versus fewer than one in ten in 2000.
- **Working-age adults (25–34):** higher-education rates rose 6.1% → 17.9% → 35.8%.
- **Elderly (65+):** the no-schooling rate fell dramatically from **55.0% → 28.1% → 14.2%**, showing that even older cohorts gained literacy, while their residual illiteracy reflects historical limitations of the pre-reform era.

## 4. Regional Perspective: Urban–Rural Convergence (Though Gaps Remain)

Education attainment is reported by urban/town/rural (city/town/rural in 2010 and 2020) sub-populations. Higher-education rates (junior college+):

| Region | 2000 | 2010 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urban / City | 11.7% | 21.5% | 28.2% |
| Town | 5.0% | 9.3% | 14.0% |
| Village / Rural | 0.5% | 2.1% | 4.9% |

No-schooling rates:
- Urban/City: 4.2% → 2.1% → 1.3%
- Town: 5.6% → 4.0% → 2.7%
- Village/Rural: 9.6% → 7.2% → 5.5%

**Achievements and remaining challenge:** literacy has been nearly universalized even in rural areas, and rural higher-education participation grew 10-fold (0.5% → 4.9%). However, the urban–rural gap in higher education remains wide — the 2020 city rate (28.2%) is ~6× the rural rate (4.9%) — even as urbanization shifted the population's centre of gravity (urban share of the 6+ population: 23.9% → 30.9% → 40.9%). Provincial tables in the dataset contain only household population and sex-ratio data (no education columns), so region-level education analysis is limited to this urban/town/rural breakdown.

## Summary of Key Achievements (2000–2020)

1. **Illiteracy virtually eliminated**: never-attended share of the 6+ population halved from ~7.7% to ~3.2%, concentrated among the elderly; the dedicated literacy-class category disappeared; school-age no-schooling dropped to 0.5%.
2. **Higher education universalized for the young**: 20–24-year-old higher-education enrolment exceeded 50% in 2020 (vs 8.6% in 2000); the total higher-education population reached 217 million (~16.5% of the 6+ population, vs 3.8%).
3. **Elite education expanded sharply**: postgraduate numbers grew ~12× to 10.8 million (0.82% of population 6+), with Master's and Doctoral tracks both expanding.
4. **Gender equality achieved in access**: female undergraduate share reached 49.4%, Master's parity at 50.2%, and the male–female higher-education gap narrowed to 0.3 percentage points.
5. **Regional catch-up**: rural no-schooling fell from 9.6% to 5.5% and rural higher-education rates grew 10-fold; urban–rural and town–rural gaps narrowed proportionally, although absolute higher-education gaps persist.

## Limitations
- Attainment categories differ across the three censuses (Literacy Class / Vocational in 2000; Master's/Doctoral and Preschool in 2020); comparability was achieved by aggregation and by restricting to ages 6+.
- The 2020 table's "ever Attended School – Total" field is treated as never-attended (verified via male+female sums).
- Provincial "region" tables contain no education variables; the regional analysis uses the urban/town/rural education tabulations only.
- Figures are census population counts (stock), not enrolment flows, so they reflect both new graduates and cohort replacement.
